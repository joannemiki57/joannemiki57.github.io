Listening to a Laser Powder Bed Fusion Print

UW–Madison FEAP Lab · Sep 2026 to present

UW–Madison, Flexible Electronics & Additive Printing Lab · DOE FIRE project (MBSE TG3) · Team lead, acoustic team

Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) builds a metal part one thin layer at a time: a recoater blade spreads powder, the laser melts the cross-section, and the cycle repeats. A microphone and a camera in the build chamber record all of it. The goal of this project, part of a DOE FIRE effort on EUROFER97 steel, is to predict the ductility and yield strength of each printed sample from those recordings. Before any modeling, the audio has to be cut into layers and every sample on the plate has to be located, and on a multi-hour build both have to be automatic.

What I built

  • Layer segmentation from audio. Acoustic equalization plus normalized cross-correlation (NCC) template matching on the recoater sound found 209 layers in a 5-hour build. Earlier RMS and watchdog approaches produced 415 to 3,039 false layer boundaries on the same recording.
  • Laser-off detection from video. Keyframe sampling with Otsu-derived brightness thresholds recovers 95 laser-off intervals from about 620,000 frames in roughly 2.5 minutes on a laptop, an independent check on the audio timeline.
  • Part map. A homography-based map that labels all 353 build-plate samples in chamber-camera frames, so each sound can be tied to the sample being printed (331 of 346 pooled detections matched to the reference grid so far).
